This is Lumia on Android (architecture section 13): `lumiakit`, the library the screens share, and `app`, the Compose layout for a phone. Read `../../docs/architecture.md` and `../../docs/plan.md` (step 7) before changing anything here.

## Layout

- `scripts/core.sh`: builds `lumia-ffi` as a shared library per ABI through `cargo-ndk` (arm64-v8a for devices, x86_64 for an Intel emulator; `--arm64` for devices only) into `lumiakit/src/main/jniLibs`, generates the Kotlin bindings with the workspace's `uniffi-bindgen` into `lumiakit/src/main/kotlin/uniffi`, and runs `scripts/tokens.py`, which writes `lumiakit/src/main/kotlin/chat/lumia/kit/Tokens.kt` from `../../assets/design/tokens.json`. Everything it produces is ignored by git; run it before any build, with `ANDROID_HOME` pointing at an SDK that has an NDK (`ANDROID_NDK_HOME` names one explicitly) and the Rust targets `aarch64-linux-android` and `x86_64-linux-android` installed.
- `lumiakit/src/main/kotlin/chat/lumia/kit`: `LumiaClient` (the UniFFI `Client` behind suspend calls on the IO dispatcher, the listener as a `Flow`), `LumiaSession` (Compose state: the snapshot, messages, DM channels and logs, the roster, the people, the connection state, presence by server from the gateway's diffs with `watchPresence` naming the servers on screen, and who is typing in the servers' channels, kept fresh by the gateway's events the way the web's queries are; `typed` says this person is typing, `typingIn` names who else is, and each is dropped when a message of theirs arrives or their time runs out, by one job that sleeps until then; a snapshot the instance refuses because the session is over, by `sessionOver`'s four codes, ends it on the phone as signing out does, and the sign-in screen says so once; a gateway loop that has ended asks for the snapshot, which says that or starts the loop again; `signOut` and that end are one leaving, by `Leaving`'s rules), `Timeline.kt` (the rules a conversation draws by, as plain values with no Compose in them: `joins` and `blockPlaces` for which messages are one block, `Typing` and `typingWords` for who is typing and how that reads; the desktop shell's rules, tested in `lumiakit/src/test`), `Preferences.kt` (what the Preferences screen chooses, as plain values tested the same way: `Appearance` and what it resolves to, `Density`, how a stored choice reads, and `Measures`, a message row's avatar and room per density), `Leaving.kt` (leaving a session, as rules tested the same way: `Leaving`, the order it happens in, one at a time and to the end; `pushMark` and `subscribedOn`, what the phone remembers of the push subscriptions it sent and which of them a sign-out takes back; `pushIsFor`, whether a push is this phone's to look into), `AppStore` (the one session and the route; reopens the saved instance, signs in from the launch intent's `LUMIA_DEV_*` extras in a debug build; keeps the two preferences beside the saved instance; `signOut`, in a scope of its own), `Design.kt` and the generated `Tokens.kt` (colours per theme, spacing, radii, type styles; `LumiaTheme` is the theme everything under it is drawn in, and outside one a colour is the system's), `Presentation.kt` (initials, the nine avatar tones, times) and `Components.kt` (avatar, notice, empty state, message row, request card, device row, composer and the typing line under it, card, the choice of one among a few, field, buttons).
- Preferences: how Lumia looks on this phone, the desktop's page without its scroll bar card (a phone's scroll indicators are the system's). **Appearance** is Light, Dark or System, which is the default and follows the phone as it changes; it is the whole app's, the sign-in and link screens too, and the system bars' icons go with it (`MainActivity`); the activity takes the phone's change of theme itself (`configChanges="uiMode"` in the manifest) instead of being made again, so the screen a person is on stays as the phone goes dark. **Messages** is Comfortable, the default and what the boards draw, or Compact: the small avatar, and half the room between blocks, between a block's messages and around a day's divider, in a channel and a personal conversation alike, and nowhere but the list of messages. The choices are the phone's and not the account's: `AppStore` keeps them in the `lumia` shared preferences as `appearance` (`light`, `dark`, `system`) and `density` (`comfortable`, `compact`), the desktop's names; nothing is sent to the instance, signing out and a session's end leave them, and a stored value this build does not know reads as the default. Each choice is a radio button to TalkBack: its label, whether it is the one chosen, and its place among the rest. No board draws the screen or a phone's way to it, so it is Profile's cards and choice, reached from the account menu: the rail's own avatar opens a menu beside it, View profile, Preferences, Devices, in the web's and the desktop's order (Devices keeps its tile on Home), and under a rule, Sign out.
- Signing out: the account menu's last item, under a rule, a button named "Sign out" to TalkBack, with no question first (the web and the desktop ask none, and nothing goes that signing in does not bring back). Its glyph is Material's `Login`, which is the picture `assets/icons/sign-out.svg` draws: an arrow into a bracket at its right. `AppStore.signOut` does it in the store's own scope, so the menu closing or the shell going cannot stop it half-way, and once: a second tap finds one under way, and the avatar opens no menu until it is done. In `Leaving`'s order, the web's: the phone's push subscription is removed from the instance while the session can still ask (`PushRegistrar.left`: the instance keeps subscriptions by device, not by session, and would go on sending the phone notices for the account), the gateway is stopped, the session revoked and its tokens forgotten (the core's `signOut`), and what the session held of the account dropped; a failure to unsubscribe or to revoke stops none of the rest. Then the sign-in screen, without the line a session the instance ended gets. The saved instance stays, Preferences' choices stay, and the identity and the device's keys stay on the phone, as on the web and the desktop: signing in again is the same device. A session the instance ended goes the same way except that it can ask the instance nothing.
- A conversation's messages, in a channel and a personal conversation alike: what one person sends close together (the same day, within five minutes of the message before) is one block, the avatar, the name and the time over the first and the rest under it a paragraph's distance apart, blocks 8 apart (`Measures.Comfortable`; Compact halves each and draws the small avatar). A row under a block's first shows no time, there being no pointer to show it under; it says whose it is and when to TalkBack before its text, and every row is one stop for it. No send is drawn before the store holds it, so there is no pending row to place. Under the composer is the line that says who is typing (protocol 6.7), in a server's channel only: three dots and "Grace Hopper is typing…", two by name, "Several people" beyond; the dots are still when the system's animations are off. The line keeps its height when nobody is typing, in a personal conversation too, where it is always empty: the composer does not move as people start and stop, and sits where the boards draw it. Its room underneath (20) is not added to the navigation bar's but takes it in, the boards ending the line at the panel's foot where the bar lies.
- `app/src/main/kotlin/chat/lumia`: the phone layout, the Quiet Split folded as the design canvas's phone boards draw it and the iOS app lays it out: `Chrome.kt` (the rail and the account menu its own avatar opens, the sidebar header and tiles, `PanelScreen`: the floating panel a pushed screen draws itself in), `Shell.kt` (the rail beside Home's conversations or a server's channels as the root, a stack of screens the back button pops; opens the conversation a tapped notification names), `Screens.kt` (conversation, channel, requests, new message, add a server: create one or join from an invite link or code with its preview first, new channel, invite people: a link with an expiry and a use limit to copy or share, over the server's invites with a revoke each, profile, preferences, devices with the revoke sheet), `SignInScreen.kt`, `LinkScreen.kt` and `MainActivity.kt` (one task; a notification's intent becomes the push target; the theme Preferences' appearance resolves to, over every screen, and the system bars' icons to match).
- Push (architecture 15.5): `lumiakit`'s `Push.kt` is what every receiver shares: `PushRegistrar.received` opens a client of its own on the saved instance and calls the core's `notificationContent` (the same call the iOS extension makes, under the same writer lock, which is in-process on Android since the standard library's file lock is unsupported there and no second process exists), then posts the message as a notification whose tap carries the channel and kind; `subscribe` sends the endpoint or token to the instance once per value and instance, remembered as a mark by platform in the `push` shared preferences (`pushMark`), and sends nothing for a phone that is signed out or signing out; `left` is what leaving a session does about it: a sign-out asks the instance to drop each platform the phone remembers subscribing as (`pushUnsubscribe`), a session the instance ended cannot, and either way the marks are forgotten, so the next sign-in, as the same account or another, subscribes again, and the notifications the phone is showing are cleared. A push that still reaches a signed-out phone (a subscription the instance could not be asked to drop) posts nothing, the generic notice neither: `received` asks the client it opens whether it is signed in. `app/.../push`: `LumiaPushService` (UnifiedPush: the distributor's endpoint and its Web Push keys as the `unified_push` subscription, under which the instance encrypts the notice; its messages as pushes), `LumiaFcmService` (FCM through the relay: the token as `fcm`, a data message naming the instance) and `PushSetup` (after sign-in, once per sign-in: the notification permission, a distributor when one is installed, FCM otherwise; registering again after a session's end is what hands the endpoint or token over again). Signing out leaves the registration with the distributor or FCM as it is. FCM is live only in a build with the Firebase project's `google-services.json` beside `app/build.gradle.kts`, which is not in the repo; without it the plugin is off and the token call fails quietly.
- Gradle: the wrapper (`./gradlew`), AGP and Kotlin from `gradle/libs.versions.toml`, `minSdk` 28 (Android 9), `compileSdk` 35; `JAVA_HOME` a JDK 17 or newer (Homebrew's `openjdk@21` here). The bindings need JNA, which the library declares.
- `scripts/release.sh [version]` builds the release (architecture section 13, "Distribution"): the core optimized and stripped for arm64 only, the APK signed with the release key named by `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PATH`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` and `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD` (unsigned without one, and named so), the version code the commit count, into `.build/dist/Lumia-<version>.apk`; `release.yml` runs it on a tag and attaches the APK to the GitHub Release. The keystore is the app's for life: Android installs an update only over the same key. `app/build.gradle.kts` takes the version and code as `-PlumiaVersion` and `-PlumiaBuild`; its own values are the development placeholders.
- `scripts/emulator.sh` creates and boots the headless emulator the smoke uses (an API 35 image for this machine's architecture); `scripts/smoke.sh` seeds a dev instance with the release CLI (the phone's account, and a second one joined through an invite the first mints, each with messages in the channel), installs the debug build, launches it once per screen with the `LUMIA_DEV_SCREEN` extra (home, server, channel, requests, devices, profile, preferences, new, add, newchannel, invite, signin, link) and screenshots each into `.build/smoke/`, which is how a change to a screen is checked. `LUMIA_CLI` names a CLI built elsewhere (a git worktree has none of its own). Then Preferences: a debug launch takes how it looks from `LUMIA_DEV_APPEARANCE` (light, dark, system) and `LUMIA_DEV_DENSITY` (comfortable, compact), for that launch only and nothing stored, so the script draws the Preferences screen dark (`preferences-dark.png`) and the channel compact (`channel-compact.png`, beside `channel.png`), and the compact channel's messages must take less room and nothing may have been kept; then, tapping as a person would, the rail's avatar opens the account menu (`account.png`), the menu leads to Preferences, Dark and Compact once chosen are the choices at the next launch (`preferences-kept.png`), System and Comfortable are chosen again so the rest is drawn as before, and the phone goes dark with the screen open, which must still be Preferences (`preferences-system-dark.png`). Then the channel as `uiautomator` reads it: each account's messages in a row must be one block (named once, the rest described for a screen reader), and the second account, typing through `lumia-cli typing`, must be named under the composer and gone after nine seconds of silence (`typing.png`, and `typing-dark.png` in the dark theme). Then push: the second account mentions the phone's account while the app is away, a launch with `LUMIA_DEV_SCREEN=notify` stands in for a push (the receivers' call, the notification posted, its content in `notification.txt`), and the notification's intent, as a tap sends it, must open the channel. Then signing out: a debug launch with `LUMIA_DEV_PUSH` subscribes the phone as `unified_push` with the endpoint named, a distributor's stand-in, which the script listens on, so a mention while the app is away must bring the instance's notice there; the account menu is drawn dark (`account-dark.png`; `account.png` is the light one, where the script checks that Sign out is a button under the rest) and closed by the back button; then avatar, Sign out, and the sign-in screen must come without the ended-session line (`signed-out.png`), the instance must list one session fewer and no other change, the phone must have forgotten the subscription and kept its instance and preferences, the next launch must be sign-in, a second mention must bring no notice to the listener, the `notify` stand-in launched signed out must find nothing and post nothing, and signing in again must subscribe again, be the same device to the instance, and get a notice for a third mention. An instance that cannot reach the script's loopback listener (one on another machine) leaves the notices unchecked, and the script's line says so. Last, the script revokes the phone's session over HTTP, as signing out everywhere does, and the app must go back to the sign-in screen and say so (`revoked.png`) with the phone's preferences still stored and the subscription it had sent forgotten, and the launch after that is the sign-in screen and no line. The emulator reaches the host's instance at `10.0.2.2`. `scripts/smoke-unifiedpush.sh` sends a real push through an installed ntfy and ntfy.sh (needs the internet and an instance started with `LUMIA_HOST=10.0.2.2`, so by hand, not CI); a dev launch with `LUMIA_DEV_*` extras keeps a session already saved for that instance rather than signing the named handle in, which is why both smokes reinstall the app first.

## Rules

- Logic lives in the Rust core; `lumiakit` holds state and presentation, `app` holds layouts. A method a screen needs goes on `lumia_client::app::App` first, then through `lumia-ffi`.
- Colours, spacing, radii and type come from `LumiaTokens`; no literal values in composables. Sentence case, typographic punctuation, no emoji in chrome.
- The screens mirror the iOS app's and the web's: the same states, wording and error handling, so a change to one is a change to all.
- Build and test: `scripts/core.sh`, then `./gradlew :lumiakit:testDebugUnitTest assembleDebug` (the unit tests are the rules in `Timeline.kt`, `Preferences.kt` and `Leaving.kt`; a rule that can be a plain value goes in one of them with a test). Run `scripts/smoke.sh` against a dev instance after a change to a screen or the session.
