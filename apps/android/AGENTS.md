This is Lumia on Android (architecture section 13): `lumiakit`, the library the screens share, and `app`, the Compose layout for a phone. Read `../../docs/architecture.md` and `../../docs/plan.md` (step 7) before changing anything here.

## Layout

- `scripts/core.sh`: builds `lumia-ffi` as a shared library per ABI through `cargo-ndk` (arm64-v8a for devices, x86_64 for an Intel emulator; `--arm64` for devices only) into `lumiakit/src/main/jniLibs`, generates the Kotlin bindings with the workspace's `uniffi-bindgen` into `lumiakit/src/main/kotlin/uniffi` (what Kotlin needs named differently is `lumia-ffi`'s `uniffi.toml`: `BodyBlock.List` is `BodyBlock.ListBlock` here, a variant named `List` hiding Kotlin's own `List` from the variants' fields), and runs `scripts/tokens.py`, which writes `lumiakit/src/main/kotlin/chat/lumia/kit/Tokens.kt` from `../../assets/design/tokens.json`. Everything it produces is ignored by git; run it before any build, with `ANDROID_HOME` pointing at an SDK that has an NDK (`ANDROID_NDK_HOME` names one explicitly) and the Rust targets `aarch64-linux-android` and `x86_64-linux-android` installed.
- `lumiakit/src/main/kotlin/chat/lumia/kit`: `LumiaClient` (the UniFFI `Client` behind suspend calls on the IO dispatcher, the listener as a `Flow`; `now` for the calls that are values only, the rail's edits and words, where they are asked), `LumiaSession` (Compose state: the snapshot, messages, DM channels and logs, the roster, the people, the connection state, presence by server from the gateway's diffs with `watchPresence` naming the servers on screen, and who is typing in the servers' channels, kept fresh by the gateway's events the way the web's queries are; `typed` says this person is typing, `typingIn` names who else is, and each is dropped when a message of theirs arrives or their time runs out, by one job that sleeps until then; a snapshot the instance refuses because the session is over, by `sessionOver`'s four codes, ends it on the phone as signing out does, and the sign-in screen says so once; a gateway loop that has ended asks for the snapshot, which says that or starts the loop again; `signOut` and that end are one leaving, by `Leaving`'s rules; a channel gained after the loop started, created, joined, added to or a server DM begun or received, is subscribed to as the snapshot that lists it is taken, once (`toSubscribe`), as the desktop shell's session does, where before it had no live events until the app started again; `previews` holds a channel opened without being in it, from Browse or a server DM just begun, which the channel screen finds there and the gateway is subscribed to; the membership calls, each answering with the snapshot where the core's does; and architecture 6.6's: `sendMessageWith`, the mentions the core's `findMentions` finds in a text channel's body sent with it and a quote, answering who it mentioned outside the channel (`channelMemberIds`, asked for as the channel opens and again when a notice says someone came or went), `dmSendWith`, `react` and `dmReact`, each taking this account's reaction away when it has it, a command the gateway refuses shown as `lastError`; `editMessage` (its mentions found again as a send's are) and `deleteMessage` through the gateway, `dmEdit` and `dmDelete` inside the encryption, an edit refused kept in `editRefusals` by message ("Not saved: …"); `roomOf`, the core's `messageRoom` for a draft while its counter shows, asked at every key of the composer and the edit field but handed to the core only for a draft long enough to be near the limit (`mayNearLimit`), the gateway's refusal matched to its edit by the command id `editMessage` answers; the Mentions lists, `loadMentions` a page at a time; `reveal`, a message found for a jump, reading further back in the store a page of 200 at a time, five pages at most; and architecture 6.9's `rail`, the snapshot's or an arrangement sent and not yet answered, `arrange`, an edit drawn at once and kept with `arrangeRail` (its snapshot taken; refused or not reached, the snapshot's rail back and `railError`), and `railNow` for the core's value-only rail calls), `Timeline.kt` (the rules a conversation draws by, a message that quotes another opening a block of its own, as plain values with no Compose in them: `joins` and `blockPlaces` for which messages are one block, `Typing` and `typingWords` for who is typing and how that reads; - Preferences: how Lumia looks on this phone, the desktop's page without its scroll bar card (a phone's scroll indicators are the system's). **Appearance** is Light, Dark or System, which is the default and follows the phone as it changes; it is the whole app's, the sign-in and link screens too, and the system bars' icons go with it (`MainActivity`); the activity takes the phone's change of theme itself (`configChanges="uiMode"` in the manifest) instead of being made again, so the screen a person is on stays as the phone goes dark. **Messages** (plan, "Display settings"), "Start from a preset, then change anything below.": **Preset**, Comfortable (the default: grouped, roomy, large, images and embeds shown), Compact (grouped, cozy, small, shown, shown) or Minimal (one line, tight, hidden, link, link), which writes its values and is not kept; the one the settings all match is shown chosen (the avatars not compared in one line), and with none "Custom: these settings match no preset." under it. Images and embeds are in the settings and the presets with no control, nothing drawing either yet. In a channel and a personal conversation alike, and nowhere but the list of messages and the composer's text. Each choice is a radio button to TalkBack: its label, whether it is the one chosen, and its place among the rest. - Signing out: the account menu's last item, under a rule, a button named "Sign out" to TalkBack, with no question first (the web and the desktop ask none, and nothing goes that signing in does not bring back). Its glyph is Material's `Logout`, mirrored with the layout's direction: an arrow going out, as the iOS app draws it with the system's. `AppStore.signOut` does it in the store's own scope, so the menu closing or the shell going cannot stop it half-way, and once: a second tap finds one under way, and the avatar opens no menu until it is done. In `Leaving`'s order, the web's: the phone's push subscription is removed from the instance while the session can still ask (`PushRegistrar.left`: the instance keeps subscriptions by device, and drops a device's only with its last session), and the phone's own registration with the distributor or FCM is given up, as the web drops the browser's subscription; the gateway is stopped, the session revoked and its tokens forgotten (the core's `signOut`), and what the session held of the account dropped; a failure to unsubscribe or to revoke stops none of the rest. Then the sign-in screen, without the line a session the instance ended gets. The saved instance stays, Preferences' choices stay, and the identity and the device's keys stay on the phone, as on the web and the desktop: signing in again is the same device, as that account or as another, which is then the only one the phone shows, holds a session for and is sent notices for. A session the instance ended goes the same way except that it can ask the instance nothing. With an instance that answers nothing the sign-out still ends, by the core's time limits: 8 seconds for the unsubscribe when the phone had subscribed and 8 for the sign-out call, about 16 in all, and stopping the gateway closes its connection whether or not the instance answers.
- The phone turned, the window resized, the font scaled: Android makes the activity again for every change of configuration but the theme's, and nothing of the session is made again with it. The store is the application's (`LumiaApp`), so there is one session and one gateway connection whatever happens to the activity, and a sign-out or a sign-in under way goes on under the new one. The activity keeps what is an activity's: the window, the permission's question (`PushSetup`), and what its launch intent said, taken once (a notification's target, the browser's return, the `LUMIA_DEV_*` look and screen: `savedInstanceState == null`); it does not tell the session it went to the background when it is only being made again. The shell keeps where the person is in the saved state (`PlaceSaver`, `StackSaver` in `Chrome.kt`: the place and the stack of screens as words), and the screens keep what was typed (`rememberSaveable`); what is waiting on an answer (`busy`, `reaching`) and what an answer held that a saved state cannot (the sign-in screen's `InstanceSummary`) are not saved, and a sign-in screen made again asks the instance again. Only values a `Bundle` takes go in `rememberSaveable`: anything else stops the app the moment it leaves the foreground.

- Push (architecture 15.5): `lumiakit`'s `Push.kt` is what every receiver shares: `PushRegistrar.received` opens a client of its own on the saved instance and calls the core's `notificationContent` (the same call the iOS extension makes, under the same writer lock, which is in-process on Android since the standard library's file lock is unsupported there and no second process exists), then posts the message as a notification whose tap carries the channel and kind; `subscribe` sends the endpoint or token to the instance once per value and instance, remembered as a mark by platform in the `push` shared preferences (`pushMark`), and sends nothing for a phone that is signed out or signing out; `left` is what leaving a session does about it: a sign-out asks the instance to drop each platform the phone remembers subscribing as (`pushUnsubscribe`), a session the instance ended cannot, and either way the marks are forgotten, so the next sign-in, as the same account or another, subscribes again, the phone's own registrations are given up (`PushRegistrar.unregister`, which the application sets to `PushSetup.unregister`, the libraries being the app's), and the notifications the phone is showing are cleared. A push that still reaches a signed-out phone (a subscription the instance could not be asked to drop) posts nothing, the generic notice neither: `received` asks the client it opens whether it is signed in. Every notification is tagged with its conversation's channel (the generic notice `lumia`) and numbered with the push's `badge`, which is what a launcher counts: Android has no badge an app sets. A read push (`kind=read`, a data message through the relay or UnifiedPush's notice) shows nothing: `readElsewhere` reads the counts again, through the session when the process has one and else a snapshot of a client of its own, and `settle` takes away the delivered notifications of conversations with nothing unread (all of them at a badge of 0) and numbers the rest with the badge; the session's `readsChanged` settles the same way whenever the counts change in the app, so a conversation read here takes its notifications with it. `app/.../push`: `LumiaPushService` (UnifiedPush: the distributor's endpoint and its Web Push keys as the `unified_push` subscription, under which the instance encrypts the notice; its messages as pushes), `LumiaFcmService` (FCM through the relay: the token as `fcm`, a data message naming the instance) and `PushSetup` (after sign-in, once per sign-in for the process however often the shell or the activity is made: the notification permission, a distributor when one is installed, FCM otherwise). A session left, signed out of or ended by the instance, gives the registration up (`PushSetup.unregister`): `UnifiedPush.unregister`, which tells the distributor and forgets the distributor chosen and the keys made for the endpoint, and FCM's `deleteToken`, each failing quietly; the next sign-in registers afresh and is handed an endpoint and keys of its own (with ntfy on the emulator: `UNREGISTER received`, the subscription removed, and another `https://ntfy.sh/up…` after signing in again). In a debug build `PushSetup.standIn`, the launch's `LUMIA_DEV_PUSH`, is an endpoint registering hands over in a distributor's place, with nothing asked of the person or of a distributor. FCM is live only in a build with the Firebase project's `google-services.json` beside `app/build.gradle.kts`, which is not in the repo; without it the plugin is off and the token call fails quietly.

## Rules

- Logic lives in the Rust core; `lumiakit` holds state and presentation, `app` holds layouts. A method a screen needs goes on `lumia_client::app::App` first, then through `lumia-ffi`.
- Colours, spacing, radii and type come from `LumiaTokens`; no literal values in composables. Sentence case, typographic punctuation, no emoji in chrome.
- The screens mirror the iOS app's and the web's: the same states, wording and error handling, so a change to one is a change to all.
- Build and test: `scripts/core.sh`, then `./gradlew :lumiakit:testDebugUnitTest assembleDebug` (the unit tests are the rules in `Timeline.kt`, `Preferences.kt`, `Leaving.kt`, `ServerSettings.kt`, `Membership.kt`, `Replies.kt`, `Body.kt`, `Emoji.kt`, `Rail.kt` and `Pictures.kt`'s `avatarPathOf`; a rule that can be a plain value goes in one of them with a test). Run `scripts/smoke.sh` against a dev instance after a change to a screen or the session, and `scripts/smoke-signout.sh` after one to signing out, the store or what the activity keeps.
