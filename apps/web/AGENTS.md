This is the Lumia web UI (architecture section 13): SolidJS 1.9 with Vite, TanStack Solid Router, Solid Query and Solid Virtual, bun as package manager and script runner. It is the UI for the browser alone; the desktop apps are the native shell in `apps/desktop`. Read `../../docs/architecture.md` and `../../docs/plan.md` (step 6) before changing anything here.

## Layout

- `src/design/`: the design system. `components.tsx` is the port of the 36 components from the design artifact plus the ones added since (notice, request card, device list, empty state; the popover with the menu, the profile card, the small form and the choice that open in one; the line of what a reply quotes (`ReplyRef`), the reply bar atop the composer's frame, the note over the composer, the @ list, the emoji picker, "Quote reply", a Mentions page's item, a message's More actions menu, its body edited where it is drawn and "Delete this message?"; a message's body (`MessageBody`, issue #18: the core's blocks drawn, the lines typed kept and a long word broken, emphasis, strong, struck and inline code, links opening in a new tab, mentions as pills, a code block that scrolls sideways and never wraps with Copy in its corner under the pointer or the keyboard ("Copied" for 1.5 s, said in a polite live region), a quote beside its rule, lists with their markers, "(edited)" after the last paragraph's words or on a line of its own; a body over fourteen lines as drawn is drawn at its first ten, fading into the row, with "Show more" and "Show less" (`aria-expanded`)) and a draft's counter (`DraftRoom`: from 90% of the limit "1,204 characters left", past it "3 characters too long" in the danger ink, told to assistive technology once as it goes over); the rail's folders, closed and open, its drag (the dashed place, the line between, the ring onto, the ghost), a menu item that opens a submenu or is in the danger ink, Folder settings with its nine tone swatches, and the note a rail edit not kept leaves) (same class names, markup and ARIA), `components.css` their stylesheet, `body.ts` the body's rules that need no page (the pieces a paragraph is drawn in from the core's runs and links, the collapse at fourteen lines to ten, the counter's words with their thousands grouped; `body.test.ts` tests them; nothing here parses Markdown), `icons.ts` the 47 glyphs of `../../assets/icons`, `emoji.ts` the picker's emoji (`emojibase-data`'s list, loaded the first time a picker opens and slimmed by a plugin in `vite.config.ts`, written in the fully-qualified form every client reacts with) and this browser's most used, and `tokens.css` is GENERATED from `../../assets/design/tokens.json` by `scripts/tokens.ts` on every build; never edit it, edit the tokens. `tokens.css` carries the dark colours under `prefers-color-scheme` for a root with no `data-theme`, so the app follows the system as it changes unless Preferences holds Light or Dark, which is `data-theme` on the root (`client/preferences.ts`, the only thing in the app that sets it); the gallery sets it too, to show a board in either, and gives the page back to the preference when left. `AppShell` is a frame the size of the window (the page never scrolls; the sidebar's list, the message list, a page's body and the aside each scroll in themselves), with the aside (the member list, a conversation's details) shown and put away by the conversation header's toggle, remembered (`preferences.ts`, `asideHidden`), and below 1100 wide sliding over the conversation's end over a shade instead, the shade or Escape putting it away and, below 720, the frame folded as the iOS boards have it: the rail and the sidebar as one screen and what is being read as a panel over them with a back button.
- `src/routes/`: the route tree (code-based TanStack router). The root elects the owning tab; `/` reopens the saved instance and goes to `/app` or `/sign-in`; `/sign-in` (instance, trust card, password or a new account; with `?invite=` from an invite link, the account is created with the code or joins after signing in, and lands in the server), `/invite/$code` (the link an invite is, on the instance: the server and the instance's trust card before anything is sent, then join, sign in or create an account; `?instance=` names the instance when the UI is not served by one), `/link` (link this browser from a native device), `/app` (the Quiet Split: a server by `?server=`, or Home by `?place=home` with `view` and `dm`; in a server, the sidebar lists the public channels the account is in, its private ones under Private when there are any, and its server DMs, Channels and Direct messages each with a plus, and invitations above the channels, and a channel Browse lists can be read before joining with `JoinBar` where the composer would be (architecture 6.8); `channels.tsx` is Browse, "Your settings in" a server, a channel's members beside it (Add people, Remove, Leave channel) and the Default channels card; `server-settings.tsx` is a server's settings page (architecture 6.7: its picture, name, default channels and retention, and "Show deleted messages" at the end of the Message history card, saved as it is turned; the core leaves deleted messages out of its reads where it is off, so the snapshot query re-reads messages when a server's setting changes), shown in the server's main pane from the menu under its name to whoever may change them (`canManage`) until a channel is chosen; `home.tsx` holds the Home sidebar, conversation, requests, new message, add a server (create one, or join from a pasted link or code; the rail's "+" lands here too), profile, preferences (appearance, and how messages are drawn: a preset of Comfortable, Compact or Minimal over layout, spacing, avatars and font size, "Custom" when the values match none; each taking effect as it is chosen) and devices; `menus.tsx` holds what opens over the frame, one at a time: the menu under a server's name with its two popovers ("New channel", and "Invite people": a link with an expiry and a use limit to copy, one mailed to an address, and the server's invites with a revoke each), the account menu on the rail's self avatar (the status, View profile, Preferences, Devices, Sign out) and the Channels heading's plus (Create a channel, Browse channels), "Add people" and "Message someone" pickers, and the profile card a member's row or a message's author opens, which in a server offers a server DM and "Message privately"). A notice (`MessageSummary.notice`) is drawn as a `NoticeLine` and joins no block. A channel's and a conversation's messages are drawn by day and, within a day, in blocks (`byBlock` in `app.tsx`: one person's messages within five minutes of each other under one name; `layout`, `spacing` and `avatars` on `MessageList` and `Message` draw them as Preferences says: one line is `time  name  text` for every message, wrapping under its text, from the same blocks), and a server's channel says who is typing under the composer (`typingIn` in `state.ts`). `timeline.tsx` draws a conversation's messages for a server's channel, a server DM and a personal DM alike (architecture 6.6): each message with the line of what it quotes over its avatar row (the "Inline replies" board; pressed, it jumps to the original, reading up to five more pages, and flashes it), its mentions as pills named as the member list names them (UTF-8 byte ranges), its reactions as chips, "(edited)", its own toolbar (Add reaction with the picker, Reply, which stays up and pressed while that message is being replied to, and More actions: Edit message for this account's own, Copy text, and Delete message for its own or, in a server's channel, anyone's where it holds `MANAGE_MESSAGES` (`canManageMessages`), which asks "Delete this message?" unless Shift is held), the row of one that names this account in the accent wash, "Quote reply" beside text selected in one message, and the "New" line where the conversation's read position was when it opened; a message of this account's is edited where it is drawn (Return saves with its mentions found again, Escape cancels, nothing left asks to delete; Up in the empty composer takes up the newest), and a refusal is said under it ("Not saved: …"; matched by the command id `editMessage` and `deleteMessage` answer, in `app.tsx`); and the composer over it, a field of lines (a textarea growing with its text to eight lines, then scrolling; Return sends, Shift+Return adds a line, nothing is sent during an input method's composition; near the limit the counter from the core's `messageRoom`, and over it Send is off, Return does nothing and the draft stays, as Save does in the edit field), with the reply bar atop its frame ("@ On", the quote's `silent` when off, in a server only and not to oneself), the @ list (a server's channel only) and the note a send leaves when someone mentioned is not in the channel (Add to the channel, Dismiss). A long body opened stays open while the conversation is (by message id, in `Timeline`), and the Mentions page draws its bodies the same way. A conversation is marked read (`createMarkRead`) while its newest message is on screen and the window has focus. Read state is the core's (`readStatesQuery` in `state.ts`, asked again after every event that can move it): unread rows in the strong ink with a count of mentions, a server's dot and count on the rail, Home's count, Mentions as a quick tile atop a server's sidebar (the board's Threads / Mentions / Saved, of which only Mentions exists) and as a row atop Home's (`mentions.tsx`, the page of `mentions`, paged as it scrolls, each item opening the channel at the message), and the badge, which is `setAppBadge` and the title's "(n) ", and which takes the shown notifications away at nothing unread (a push names no conversation, so they cannot be taken away one conversation at a time). `rail.tsx` is the rail's order and folders (architecture 6.9, the "Server folders" boards): the snapshot's `rail` drawn by `ServerRail` (Home first and + last, neither moving nor taking a drop; servers, Home, folders and + rounded squares, the account's own avatar round), a closed folder as its first four servers small in its tone with its servers' mentions added up or the unread dot, an open one as its glyph and its servers in a column of its tone, open or closed kept per browser (`localStorage`, `lumia:rail-open`), and every edit by the core's rules (`railEdit`): drawn at once, said in the rail's polite live region ("Acme moved to position 3", "… moved into Work", "… moved out of Work"), kept whole with `arrangeRail`, and on a refusal the rail as it was put back and a note beside the rail says why. A drag is the rail component's, with pointer events (not touch): over a tile its top or bottom quarter is between (a line in the accent) and its middle half onto (a server: `merge`; a folder: `intoFolder`; a server in an open folder: into that folder before it), a folder drags whole and only between, Escape cancels. A tile's menu (right-click, Shift+F10, the context menu key; `railMenu` over the frame): a server's Move up, Move down, New folder, Move to folder › and Remove from folder, a folder's Move up, Move down, Folder settings… (`folderSettings`: a name with the folder's words as its placeholder, the tone, Cancel and Save) and Delete folder. A folder with no name reads as the core's words for its servers (`railFolderWords`). Tiles that have not changed are handed back as they were, so a snapshot read again keeps the rail's elements. `auth-page.tsx` is the page of the Sign in board, shared by sign-in, link, invite and the other-tab screen. `/design` is the gallery that rebuilds the design canvas's boards from the components with their sample data (the Quiet Split, "Inline replies" at its 960 by 800, Home, the phones, Server folders); it is how a change to the system is checked, in both themes.
- A build in `--mode instance` (`bun run build:instance`) is the one an instance serves: `SERVED_BY_INSTANCE` in `state.ts` makes the page's own origin the instance and hides the address field.
- `src/client/`: the bridge to the core. `worker.ts` runs `lumia-wasm`'s `Client` in a dedicated worker (OPFS sync access handles exist only there) and answers RPC calls, adding to every message it hands the page (`messages`, `olderMessages`, `mentions`, `dmMessages`) its body read into blocks by the core (`parseBody`, kept by text and mention ranges so a conversation read again is not parsed again), so the page parses nothing; `bridge.ts` is its typed page side; `state.ts` holds the one bridge, the account snapshot and messages as Solid Query queries that the gateway's events invalidate (a channel's are the store's newest pages and, above them, what the instance gave of its history from before the store begins: `loadOlderMessages` reads a page further back from the store while it has more and then from the instance, as the reader nears the oldest message shown, kept for the page's life and not stored, as the desktop shell does), and presence by server from the gateway's diffs (`watchPresence` names the server on screen, re-sent on every welcome). `preferences.ts` is what the Preferences page holds, this browser's and not the account's: light, dark or as the system is, and the six display settings with the presets that write them (`PRESETS`, `presetOf`; plan step 9, "Display settings"), kept in `localStorage` under `lumia:preferences` (anything not understood reads as that setting's default), applied before the first render and followed from another tab. The font size is `--msg-scale` on the root, which the message rows and the composer multiply their sizes by; the gallery sets it back to 1. `preferences.test.ts` is the rules' unit tests (`bun run test`). `autoscroll.ts` is autoscroll with the middle button held over any pane that scrolls, on every browser, by the desktop shell's numbers, with the browser's own (and Linux's middle-click paste) cancelled over a pane. `passkeys.ts` is the browser's own WebAuthn ceremony between the instance's options and its check (the Devices page makes passkeys, the sign-in page uses them). `pkg/` is GENERATED by `scripts/wasm.ts`.
- `e2e/run.ts`: the browser suite (Playwright, Chromium and WebKit, persistent contexts). It needs a dev instance on :4000 (`LUMIA_INSTANCE` names another) and the release CLI, which it uses as the other device (seeding, approving the link, asking for and answering a DM, writing the message a member is not offered to edit or delete, minting the invite a fresh browser opens and creates an account from, mentioning the browser's account once it has gone away and while its Mentions page is open, writing 1,050 messages for the history step to scroll back through, trying a spent handoff token, making the rail step's three servers). The rail step moves, makes folders and names them by the menus, drags with the mouse (onto a server, a folder between, one put back by Escape) and reads the rail back from the instance's `GET /account/snapshot`. The suite runs a push service of its own on :4919 for the Web Push step (Chromium only): a `pushManager` stand-in hands the app the suite's endpoint and keys, the app's own path registers the subscription, and the instance's push is decrypted here; the instance accepts that plain-http loopback endpoint only under `push_loopback_endpoints` (its dev and test configs).
- `../../assets` is the public root (`publicDir`): `/fonts`, `/brand`, `/icons`, `/design` resolve to the shared assets.
- Logic lives in the Rust core (`apps/core`, built to wasm and run in a worker); this app holds the UI and a thin bridge to that worker. Nothing here decides a protocol or storage question.

## Rules

- Solid, not React: props are read lazily (never destructured), `class` not `className`, `for` not `htmlFor`, `style` objects use CSS property names, lists are `<For>`, conditions `<Show>`.
- The screens mirror the desktop shell's (`apps/desktop/src/ui`) and the iOS app's: the same states, wording and error handling. A screen is checked against its board by looking at both, in light and dark, at the board's size.
- What the boards draw that the product has nothing behind (search, threads, saved, attachments, pins, calls, role tags, the composer's formatting, emoji and mention buttons) is not drawn in the app; a control that does nothing is worse than none. The gallery keeps drawing them, since it rebuilds the boards. Plan step 9 lists them.
- The design system's components are the vocabulary; a screen that needs a new element adds it to `components.tsx` with its CSS beside the others, named `lu-*`, and shows it in the gallery.
- Colours, spacing, radii, shadows and sizes come from tokens (`var(--…)`); no literal values in screen CSS.
- A field being typed in draws no focus ring (Ryan, 2026-11-13): its caret and border say it is focused. Buttons and links keep the ring for the keyboard.
- Sentence case everywhere, typographic punctuation, no emoji in chrome; the brand book in the design artifact and `assets/README.md` is the reference.
- `bun run check` (tokens, wasm, typecheck), `bun run test` and `bun run build` must pass; CI runs them. Run `bun run e2e` against a dev instance after a change to a screen or the bridge.
- Events from the worker are plain objects with camelCase fields (`rename_all_fields` on the Rust enum); the TypeScript `ClientEvent` in `bridge.ts` must match the Rust `ClientEvent` in `lumia-wasm/src/client.rs`, and `BodyBlock` and `MessageRoom` the Rust `DisplayBlock` and `Room` in `lumia-core/src/body/mod.rs`.
